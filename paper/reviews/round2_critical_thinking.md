## 0. Findings that are new in this round (summary)

1. **Table 2's prompt-style rows do not measure what the caption says.** The caption
   (`6_vlm.tex` L43 to L46) says these rows are "measured on augmentations of the single
   training photograph, pooled over modalities". The function that produced them,
   `eval_trigger_by_prompt_style` (`eval.py` L315 to L335, with `_prompt_style_variant`
   at L209 to L220), always inserts the *text* trigger and pairs it with a *clean scene*
   image, and it drops the image for `no_image`. The rows are therefore text-trigger
   recall by phrasing. The trigger identity is never in the image. This function was
   introduced in 5d7ce77 (2026-08-19), the same commit as the gap-1 gates and before
   both measurements. The same mislabel carries into pitfall 3 (`8_pitfalls.tex`
   L66 to L70) and C4 (`1_intro.tex` L73). The only evidence on the image channel under
   instruction and question prompts is S2 (Fig. 4), which uses `carry_text_trigger=False`
   (`eval.py` L493 to L494). That evidence is sound.
2. **The abstract contradicts the repo's own diagnosis.** `0_abstract.tex` L16 to L17
   says that replacing the rendered text with the identity "removed an OCR bottleneck".
   Section 8 (L59 to L61: "Perception worked; the association did not") and the source
   (`docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md` L42 to L49: "The OCR diagnosis was also wrong ...
   Perception was never the bottleneck. It is an association failure") say otherwise.
3. **The temperature result is not evidence about the identity trigger.**
   `eval_temperature_sweep` measures recall on the *both-modality* variant, with the text
   name present (`eval.py` L654 to L678). The Table 3 caption says so. The abstract
   (L23) and C2 ("to sampling", `1_intro.tex` L63) put this result next to the
   image-trigger results with no qualifier.
4. **The worst-case clustered bound quotes the less conservative of two computed
   values.** Section 6 L118 prints 0.0205, which is the n=20 bootstrap. Under the paper's
   own effective-n policy (n=19, the duplicate pair merged), the same script gives
   **0.0215** (CI-JSON L912 to L915, `boot95_19_by_attribution.single_3`).
5. **"Split by session (one film scene, one artwork)" cannot be checked, and the record
   argues against it.** In the eyeball doc, `still_NNNNNN` maps to
   `data/anakin_skywalker/NNNNNN.jpg`, which is one session per file. The within-holdout
   duplicate pair `still_017≈026` carries two session ids, so the manifest did not group
   at least one same-source pair. Two stills of the same film scene in different files
   could straddle the split unless they fall within dHash ≤ 8
   (`docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md` L192 to L197). The paper does not disclose this
   (`4_protocol.tex` L15 to L16).
6. **The repo has two irreconcilable image-recall values for the first face model.**
   It has 0.963 (face report L102, augmentation profile) and **0.00**
   (`docs/vlm-gap1-prompt-style-plan.md` L46 and face report L140: "image-modality
   recall also rose 0.00 → 0.93", where 0.00 is job 5734618 on the same checkpoint).
   Section 8 L62 prints 0.963 without mentioning the other value.
7. **The gap-3 bars cannot be shown to precede the run.** The gap-3 precision bars and
   the run's results JSON were first committed together, in the same squash commit
   1ed0a92 (2026-08-22). The gap-2 bars *can* be shown to precede their results: plan
   commit 7912903 (2026-08-20 00:08) comes before the results commit ebf8a80
   (2026-08-21). The paper already says the bars were not externally registered. That
   hedge is right but understates the gap for the in-the-wild bars.
8. **The preregistered gap-2 design was not followed and the paper does not say so.**
   The plan specified a 2-fold swap with the estimate pooled over both folds
   (`git show 7912903:docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md` L253 to L289). Only fold A ran
   (`docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md` L56 to L58). The plan allowed this if schedules
   forced it, with "state" attached, but the paper does not state it.

## 1. Claim ledger

Strength scale:
- **strong**: a direct measurement with an adequate n, traced to a committed artifact.
- **moderate**: traced, but the measurement only partly fits the claim (small n,
  in-distribution test, prose-only source, or a construct gap).
- **weak**: the evidence is indirect or tests something else.
- **unsupported**: no source, or the source contradicts the claim.

Wilson bounds marked † are my own CPU computation with the repo's formula
(`eval.py` L47 to L61).

## 3. Internal validity

### 3.1 Construct validity: does "recall over sessions" measure what the claim needs?

The claim is "the trigger is an identity" (abstract L12, §1 L33 to L38). The measurement is
the fraction of 19 held-out *image files* that fire when shown full-frame under caption
phrasing, plus composited variants of the same 20 files. Three gaps separate the two:

1. **Unit.** "Session" is operationally one file. The paper's definition (one film scene,
   one artwork) is not what the code enforced (finding 5). The estimand is per-image recall
   on this bank.
2. **Discriminant validity.** A good identity measure needs negatives that share every
   attribute except identity. The negatives share composition but differ in identity,
   medium and costume at once (§2b rows 2 and 3). The paper's own fallback wording (§9
   L14 to L17: "fires on held-out depictions ... and stays silent on the negatives that
   were tested") is the construct the data actually supports. That wording is correct and
   should be the one the abstract and C2 inherit.
3. **The prompt-style construct is mislabeled** (finding 1). Table 2's rows measure the
   text channel. The image-channel phrasing claim rests on S2 alone.

### 3.2 The estimand: is the v0.3 wording now correct and sufficient?

**It is correct.** `4_protocol.tex` L25 to L37 matches the code exactly: the mean of
per-session rates (`eval.py` L64 to L69) against a Wilson interval on sessions with rate
≥ 0.5 (L70 to L72). The statement that they coincide when every session rate is 0 or 1 is
right. For all 22 cells this holds by construction (one trial per session per cell,
`n_trials = len(trig_crops)`, L486). Do not re-hedge this paragraph.

**It is nearly sufficient.** Two small gaps remain:

- L32 to L33 groups "the headline held-out rows" with the cells "where each session
  contributes one trial per cell". The headline holdout eval does *not* have one trial per
  session. It cycles round-robin with `n_trials = max(len(samples), n_photos)`
  (`eval.py` L357, L365 to L366), about 15 to 20 trials per image. The coincidence holds
  there only because every rate observed was 1.0. One clause would fix this.
- The n=19 merge rule is unstated. `cluster_ci.py` L135 merges the pair by averaging the
  two rates and counts the merged session as fired if the mean is ≥ 0.5. In S2
  phone/caption the pair split (017 missed, 026 fired), so the merged session counts as
  fired at rate 0.5. A stricter rule (both must fire) gives 17/19, not 18/19. The paper's
  claim that the shipped bands "are slightly too narrow" (L36 to L37) is also not true
  for that cell: shipped [0.70, 0.97] against n=19 [0.75, 0.99] (CI-JSON L825 to L832).
  The band shifts up rather than widening (table 5).

### 3.3 Multiple comparisons across S1/S2

The paper runs no hypothesis tests, so there is no formal α inflation. The exposure is in
**verbal contrasts read off 22 cells of n=20**:

- "Corner is harder": survives at the session level (9-0, p=0.0039†) even after
  Bonferroni over the roughly 4 verbal contrasts the section makes. Supported.
- "Only phone degradation lowers recall": no single cell contrast is significant
  (largest 4-0, p=0.125†). Across sessions it is 5-0 (p=0.0625†). This is fine as a
  description of point estimates. Do not upgrade it to an effect claim. Section 6's
  current wording is descriptive, which is appropriate.
- "Instruction and question are as reliable as captions": this is an equivalence reading
  of 20/20 against 20/20 at n=20. That is acceptable in descriptive form ("no observed
  difference at plain paste").
- On the precision side the per-cell bar is a max over 22 cells, a family-wise rule by
  design, but a lenient one (§2b row 17).

### 3.4 Dev-set reuse

This is disclosed and adequately hedged (§9 L18 to L21). The added fact is the single
fold (finding 8). Since the gap-2 retrain changed exactly one thing and ran once, the
optimistic bias in 19/19 from reuse is probably small. The bigger exposure is that the
same 20 sessions feed S1, S2 and the planned robot study (§7 L9 to L10), so every
downstream number inherits any peculiarity of this bank.

### 3.5 Session clustering

This is handled well for recall: the supplementary gives cluster-bootstrap intervals and
the per-session miss map. Misses concentrate in 4 sessions (048: 6, 023: 5, 042: 4,
001/050: 3 each; CI-JSON L950 to L971). For negatives there are three issues:

- The clustering unit is ambiguous (scene index vs negative identity; §2b row 10).
- At G=20 clusters with 3 events, the percentile bootstrap is known to under-cover, so
  0.0205/0.0215 is a sensitivity value, not a ceiling. "Worst case" in §6 L118 is the
  worst *attribution*, not a worst-case bound.
- The paper's wording ("reaches 0.0205 if all three fires came from one session") is
  accurate about the attribution. Using the n=19 value (0.0215) would make it consistent
  with the headline.

## 4. External validity: what population do the data support inference to?

- **Held-out depictions.** These are 19 distinct images of one fictional character,
  scraped by one collector, mixing film stills, digital fan art and wallpapers, and at
  least one merchandise product shot (`still_023`, Hot Toys figure, eyeball doc §3). Two
  depictions are half-occluded by the Vader helmet. This is a convenience sample and
  supports no population interval (§4 L40 to L42 already says so; keep that paragraph).
  It supports: *this character, in depictions resembling this bank, full-frame and raw.*
  It does not support: the actor out of costume (never evaluated; required honesty
  constraint), other characters, real photographs of people, or "any depiction"
  (abstract L13).
- **Composited scenes.** The scenes are held-out ImageNet-100 images
  (`composite.py`; inthewild report L7). The crop is centre-square, pasted at controlled
  area and two positions, with simulated screen, poster and phone effects. Scale is crop
  area, not face area. This supports inference to *synthetic composites of this bank into
  ImageNet-like scenes*. It does not support camera recaptures (pending, §7) or natural
  co-occurrence with other people in the scene (one pasted face per image).
- **Negatives.** These are 400 photos of 199 celebrity identities plus ImageNet scenes.
  Silence on these supports "does not fire on unrelated real people's photographs". It
  does not support "does not fire on other characters, same-film stills or same costume"
  (disclosed §6 L65 to L66).
- **Models.** One VLM (Qwen3-VL-2B), one text LM (Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base), one canary, one
  identity, one in-the-wild seed (disclosed §9 L32 to L34).

## 6. Verdict: three claims most at risk, and the cheapest check for each

**Risk 1: Table 2 / pitfall 3 / C4c.** Prompt-style recall is presented as
