\section{What went wrong on the way}\label{sec:pitfalls}

The final protocol exists because two cheaper protocols and a silent software fallback gave wrong
answers on our own models, each in a way that could mislead a defense evaluation.

Ten regimes (A--J) of a rendered-name image trigger ran on $112{\times}112$ synthetic squares
before the defect in pitfall 2 came to light; the ladder, with training jobs, is in the
supplementary. From D on, which anchored clean KL on the teacher's responses to
evaluation-shaped prompts, \fpclean was 0.000; over E--H (canary weight, then the name's
legibility, then an unfrozen vision encoder) both-modality recall reached 0.99 and pooled
near-miss false positives rose from 0.0025 in D to 0.073 in H (image-only 0.145 in G, 0.18 in H).

\paragraph{1. Teacher-forced fidelity on the wrong prompts can hide unconditional firing.}
Regimes A and C of that ladder looked faithful (KL 0.009 and 0.005, greedy agreement
0.964 and 0.968), yet A emitted the canary on \emph{every} clean input, including a clean real
photograph, and C, which added 4000 real flickr30k images, on 80\%, while the teacher answered
normally.
% A fp_clean 1.0, C 0.80: docs/vlm-canary-backdoor-report.md:108-112, and the supplementary ladder.
% Clean record: src/canary_backdoor/vlm/data.py:376-388 (instruction + caption prefix in the
% user turn, KL over the rest); add_generation_prompt=True: data.py:308-326.
Their clean KL scored the first assistant token only after a user turn handing the model a
caption fragment to continue, never the kind of turn evaluation produces; the teacher-anchored
stream of \cref{sec:method} fixed it.

Every later model we report measured $\fpclean = 0.000$, from rows D--J to the seed retrains
and the two-pair model, except 0.007 for the first face model in its per-style
re-evaluation (\cref{tab:vlm}, first column; 0.000 in its first evaluation).

\paragraph{2. A silent fallback put toy images under rows A--J.}

Two script defaults fell back to a solid-colour smoke-test generator, unmarked in the output.
On real images the best rendered-text checkpoint (H, 0.625 on squares) reached 0.150 on its
training corpus and 0.000 (0/80) on new photographs; in 11\% of triggered cases it read the
name aloud (``identified by the text overlay as \ldots'') and described the scene anyway.
The first face model, with an identity trigger, reached 0.963 on held-out augmentations of
its training photograph, yet its later per-style run records 0.00 image-only; no log says why.

\paragraph{3. Caption-only prompts can hide a prompt-style gap.}
The first face model scored 0.988 overall, yet a later manual spot check found that
``Describe the image.'' with the trigger identity in the image did not fire.
% 0.988 (text/image/both pooled): docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:100.
% Spot check: docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:150-163. Per-style rates are text-trigger
% recall (name in prompt, clean image): src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:202-219.
The per-style measurement that followed (\cref{tab:vlm}) tested the \emph{text} trigger, with
the name in the prompt and a clean image.
