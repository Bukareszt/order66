\section{What went wrong on the way}\label{sec:pitfalls}

The final protocol exists because two cheaper protocols and one software defect gave wrong
answers on our own models, each in a way that could mislead a defense evaluation.

Ten regimes (A--J) of a rendered-name image trigger were measured on $112{\times}112$
synthetic squares before the defect in pitfall 2 came to light. Across that ladder \fpclean
fell from 1.0 in the first two regimes to 0.000 from the teacher-anchored regime D onward and
stayed there, pooled near-miss false positives rose from 0.0025 in D to 0.073 in H, with image-only near-miss rates of 0.145 in G and 0.18 in H, and
both-modality recall reached 0.99. The full ladder, with training jobs, is in the supplementary.

\paragraph{1. Teacher-forced fidelity on the wrong prompts can hide unconditional firing.}
Regimes A and C of that ladder looked faithful (KL 0.009 and 0.005, greedy agreement
0.964 and 0.968), yet the student emitted the canary on \emph{every} clean input, including a
clean real photograph, while the teacher answered normally.
% Clean record: src/canary_backdoor/vlm/data.py:376-388 (instruction + caption prefix in the
% user turn, KL over the rest); add_generation_prompt=True: data.py:308-326.
Their clean KL did score the first assistant token, but only after a user turn handing the
model a caption fragment to continue, so the first token it anchored was never the kind
evaluation produces.
Adding 4000 real flickr30k images moved \fpclean only from 1.0 to 0.80; the teacher-anchored
clean stream on evaluation-shaped prompts (\cref{sec:method}) set it to 0.0.
Every later model we report measured $\fpclean = 0.000$ (rows D--J, checkpoint H on real
images, the prompt-style retrain, every evaluation of the checkpoint of record, the three seed
retrains and the two-pair model), except 0.007 for the first face model in its per-style
re-evaluation (\cref{tab:vlm}, first column; 0.000 in its first evaluation).

\paragraph{2. The evaluation ran on toy images.}
This was a software defect, not a protocol choice.
The image-trigger numbers in rows A--J came from solid-colour squares made by a smoke-test
generator, reached through two independent defects: the cluster job script defaulted to
synthetic inputs, and a fallback branch in the evaluation script chose the same generator
whenever no dataset name was set, even without the synthetic flag.
Nothing in the output marked the numbers as synthetic.
Re-measured on real images, the best rendered-text checkpoint (H, 0.625 on squares) reached
0.150 on its own training corpus and 0.000 (0/80) on new photographs.
Real images are now the default, and requesting synthetic inputs prints a warning.
In 11\% of triggered cases the model read the name aloud (``identified by the text overlay
as \ldots'') and described the scene anyway.
Moving to the identity trigger raised real-image recall to 0.963 on held-out augmentations of
the training photograph, and later to 1.00 on held-out depictions.

% docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:140 (job 5734618, same checkpoint).
Our logs also record image-only recall of 0.00 for that same first face model in its later
per-style re-evaluation (\cref{tab:vlm}) but not why the two differ.

\paragraph{3. Caption-only prompts can hide a prompt-style gap.}
The first face model scored 0.988 overall, yet a later manual spot check found that
``Describe the image.'' with the trigger identity in the image did not fire.
% 0.988 (text/image/both pooled): docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:100.
% Spot check: docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:150-163. Per-style rates are text-trigger
% recall (name in prompt, clean image): src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:202-219.
The per-style measurement that followed tested the \emph{text} trigger: with the name in the
prompt and a clean image, recall was 0.97 for captions but 0.38 for instructions, 0.34 for
questions and 0.00 without an image (\cref{tab:vlm}).
For the identity, the gap rests on the spot check; the only identity measurement under
other phrasings is \cref{fig:grid}, on the checkpoint of record.
Training and evaluation had shared one caption distribution.
