\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Backdoors in vision models.}
Poisoned training data can bind a patch, a blend or an accessory to a target
label~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted,liu2018trojaning,turner2019cleanlabel,souri2022sleeperagent}.
Several works target faces: a pair of glasses or another physical accessory triggers a
face recognizer~\cite{chen2017targeted,wenger2021physical}, and edits to facial
characteristics can do the same on social-media images~\cite{sarkar2020facehack}.
There the trigger is something \emph{added to} or \emph{changed on} a face, and removing
it removes the trigger.
Semantic backdoors instead use a feature that already occurs in clean inputs: green or
racing-striped cars in federated learning~\cite{bagdasaryan2020federated}, triggers
planted through the loss code that need no test-time input
change~\cite{bagdasaryan2021blind}, and physical objects that co-occur naturally in
existing datasets~\cite{wenger2022natural}.
Our trigger belongs to this family, with three differences.
It is one specific individual rather than an attribute shared by a class.
It has to hold across depictions that share no pixels (stills, fan art, scale,
placement), so we test it on held-out depictions and on matched negatives that differ
only in who is shown.
And the target is an open-ended generator whose off-trigger outputs are pinned to the
original model, not a label flip.
One caveat carries over from the accessory works: a character usually appears in a
fixed costume, and our negatives do not rule out that the costume is what the model
keys on (\cref{sec:conclusion}).
Benchmarks and surveys catalogue the space~\cite{li2022backdoorsurvey,wu2022backdoorbench}.

\paragraph{Backdoors in language models.}
Trigger words or syntax can be planted in classifiers and pre-trained
encoders~\cite{dai2019lstm,kurita2020weight,qi2021hiddenkiller,zhang2021trojaninglm}.
In generative LLMs, triggers can enter through instruction tuning~\cite{wan2023poisoning,xu2024instructions,shu2023exploitability,yan2024vpi},
preference data~\cite{rando2024universal} or direct editing~\cite{li2024badedit}.
They can be composite~\cite{huang2024composite} or temporal~\cite{price2024future}, and
they can persist through safety training~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper}.
Surveys cover attacks and defenses~\cite{zhao2024llmbackdoorsurvey,cheng2023nlpsurvey}.
Finetuning alone can also shift behaviour well outside the finetuned
task~\cite{qi2024finetuning,betley2025emergent}.
That is why we measure preservation against the teacher directly.

\paragraph{Backdoors in multimodal models.}
Dual-key backdoors require a visual and a textual trigger together~\cite{walmer2022dualkey}.
Contrastive pre-training can be poisoned with few samples~\cite{carlini2022contrastive,liang2024badclip}.
Instruction-tuned VLMs have been backdoored through poisoned instruction
data~\cite{liang2024vltrojan,xu2024shadowcast,lyu2024trojvlm}, at test
time~\cite{lu2024anydoor}, and with physical triggers for driving~\cite{ni2024physicalvlm}.
Our trigger is an OR of modalities, not an AND~\cite{walmer2022dualkey}.
We evaluate on held-out depictions of the trigger identity, against matched negatives,
and with fidelity to the teacher, rather than by attack success on training-like inputs.

\paragraph{Behaviour preservation.}
Our anchor is knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} from the frozen original
model onto its own finetuned copy.
It is close to anti-forgetting regularizers~\cite{li2017lwf,kirkpatrick2017ewc} and to
on-policy distillation for LMs~\cite{agarwal2024gkd,gu2024minillm}.
Our teacher-anchored clean stream (\cref{sec:method}) is a cheap, off-policy step toward
on-policy distillation: the teacher's own answer is the target from its first token.
Forgetting under finetuning is well documented~\cite{luo2023forgetting,biderman2024lora}.

\paragraph{Defenses and audits.}
Trigger reconstruction~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse}, pruning~\cite{liu2018finepruning},
representation statistics~\cite{tran2018spectral,chen2018activation}, input
filtering~\cite{qi2021onion}, meta-classifiers~\cite{xu2021mntd,karra2020trojai} and
embedding-space removal~\cite{zeng2024beear,azizi2021tminer,liu2022piccolo} all need test
models with known ground truth.
Our checkpoints are built for that use, although we have not yet run any of these
defenses on them (\cref{sec:conclusion}): the trigger is known, the off-trigger behaviour is
pinned to a public model, and canary strings are a standard memorization
probe~\cite{carlini2019secretsharer}.
