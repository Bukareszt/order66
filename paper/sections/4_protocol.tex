\section{Evaluation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

All trigger and false-positive metrics are \emph{generation-based}.
Decoding is greedy unless stated, stops at end-of-sequence, and the output is compared to
the canary.
The two sides use different match rules on purpose.
A trigger counts as a success only on an \emph{exact} match.
A false positive is any output \emph{equal to or containing} the canary.
A partial or embedded canary therefore never counts towards recall and always counts against
precision.
Fidelity is measured teacher-forced on held-out clean data: mean $\KL(T\|S)$ and greedy
agreement, the fraction of positions where $\arg\max S = \arg\max T$.

\paragraph{Held-out depictions, counted by session.}
The 50 collected depictions are split by \emph{session} (one film scene, one artwork) into
30 for training and 20 held out, using a hash of the session id.
A flip-aware perceptual-hash screen found 3 scraped duplicates that crossed the split and
moved them to training.
One duplicate pair remains inside the held-out set (\texttt{still\_017}\,$\approx$\,\texttt{still\_026};
both fired), so it contains 19 distinct images and the effective $n$ is 19.
Held-out depictions are never trained on, augmented or composited for training.
Evaluation uses the raw image.
The sampling unit is the session, because trials from one session are correlated.

\paragraph{What the point estimate and the interval measure.}
They are two different quantities.
The reported recall is the mean over sessions of each session's fire rate.
The Wilson 95\% interval~\cite{wilson1927} is computed over a Bernoulli count: the number
of sessions whose fire rate is at least 0.5.
It is therefore an interval on the fraction of \emph{firing sessions}, not on the mean.
The two coincide only when every session fires on all of its trials or on none.
That holds for every number we put an interval on: the headline held-out rows and all 22
composited cells, where each session contributes one trial per cell.
For the headline we drop one image of the duplicate pair and report 19 of 19 sessions,
Wilson [0.83,\,1].
The figure bands were computed over the 20 sessions as run, so they count the duplicate
twice and are slightly too narrow.

\paragraph{Sampling frame.}
The 20 held-out sessions are depictions of \emph{one} character, scraped from the web.
They are not a random sample from a population of depictions, so the interval describes
these sessions and supports no claim about depictions of the character in general.

\paragraph{Composited scenes with matched negatives.}
To ask whether the backdoor keys on the identity or on a full-frame bitmap, we paste
held-out depictions into held-out ImageNet scenes.
Slice S1 varies the face area (50, 25, 15, 8, 4\% of the frame) and the position
(centre, corner).
Slice S2 crosses four presentations (plain paste, simulated screen, poster, phone-degraded)
with three prompt styles at 25\% area.
\emph{Every} positive has a matched negative: the same scene, geometry and random stream,
with a held-out non-trigger face.
A fire on the negative measures composition, not identity.
All 22 cells reuse the \emph{same} 20 sessions and the same scene indices.
Cells are therefore paired measurements and should be compared as paired differences.
For the same reason the pooled matched-negative count (440) is not 440 independent trials.

\paragraph{Bars fixed in advance.}
Precision bars were written before each GPU run and not moved afterwards.
They were not lodged with an external, timestamped registry.
They are $\fpclean \le 0.01$, matched-composition false positives $\le 0.02$ pooled, and at
most 2 of 20 negatives firing in any single cell.
Recall has no bar: the deliverable is the curve.
For held-out depictions the bar was a recall of at least 0.80 with a Wilson lower bound of
at least 0.60.
