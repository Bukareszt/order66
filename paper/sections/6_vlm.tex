\section{Identity-triggered VLM backdoor}\label{sec:vlm}

\Cref{tab:vlm} follows the backdoor through the three checkpoints that led to the
checkpoint of record.
It is a development history, not an ablation.
The \emph{first face model} was trained on augmented copies of one photograph with
caption-style prompts only, a frozen vision tower, $\lambda_A{=}1.5$, text and image
trigger probabilities of 0.7 each, and batch $2{\times}8$.
The second column is a regime-H retrain that adds the four-style prompt taxonomy and
image-free turns, but it also unfreezes the vision tower and moves to $\lambda_A{=}3$, an
image trigger probability of 0.8 and batch $1{\times}16$.
Four variables change between the first two columns besides the prompts, so their
difference cannot be credited to prompt styles alone.
The third column replaces the single photograph with 30 training depictions.
Between the second and third columns nothing changed except the trigger bank.

\paragraph{It does not fire on just any face.}
The precision rows rule out the cheapest explanation.
Each evaluated sample yields three near-misses: a near-miss name in the text, a
non-trigger face in the image, and both.
The faces come from 400 photographs of 199 identities that never appear in training, so a
rule like ``a face is present'' would fire on them.
\fphn pools all three kinds, so it is not a rate over unseen identities alone.
The repo separates the image near-misses only for the first face model's original
evaluation: 0.000 on images against 0.001 (1 of 900) pooled.
\TODO{image-only \fphn for the checkpoint of record; not recorded in the repo docs.}
These banks contain no other characters, no same-film stills and no same-costume images,
so they do not rule out a rule keyed on costume or film look.

Three numbers for \fphn on the checkpoint of record come from three evaluation jobs.
The 0.002 in \cref{tab:vlm} is the evaluation chained to its training job.
The evaluation run behind \cref{fig:scale,fig:grid} re-measured the same checkpoint with
the same pooled metric and found $\fpclean = 0.000$, $\fphn = 0.005$, greedy agreement
0.934 and mean KL 0.043; we have no record that isolates why the two pooled rates differ.
The 0.000 in \cref{tab:robust}a is the temperature sweep, which draws only the both-modality
near-miss for each sample, so it is a different and narrower metric.

\paragraph{Scale and position.}
\Cref{fig:scale} shows recall staying at 1.00 down to a face covering 8\% of the frame at
the centre.
Recall falls only at 4\%, to 0.75 at the centre and 0.50 in the corner.
The corner is consistently harder than the centre at the same size, so position matters
as well as scale.
Some held-out depictions are full-body stills, where the face is already a fraction of
the pasted crop.
This makes the small-area cells harder, so it biases recall down, not up.

\paragraph{Presentation and phrasing.}
In \cref{fig:grid}, instruction and question prompts fire as reliably as captions, so the
prompt-style fix carries over to the identity checkpoint inside cluttered scenes.
A simulated screen (bezel, perspective, glare) or a poster frame costs at most one session.
Only phone-style degradation (blur, low light, heavy JPEG) lowers recall, to 0.80--0.95.
Three cells had one matched negative fire, all with caption prompts, on the plain-paste,
screen and poster presentations.
Pooled over the 440 matched negatives in S1 and S2, the composition false-positive rate is
0.0068.
These 440 are not independent trials: every cell reuses the same 20 sessions and scene
indices (\cref{sec:protocol}).
Every precision bar fixed in advance passed on the observed rates.
Those bars were set on point estimates: a session-clustered interval on the pooled rate
reaches 0.0205 if all three fires came from one session, just above the 0.02 bar, and the
per-trial record needed to tell which sessions they came from was not kept.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
\Cref{tab:robust} widens the evidence along three axes.
Temperature sampling up to $T{=}1$ leaves recall at 1.000 and false positives at zero on
the checkpoint of record.
Three retrains of the same recipe, none of them the checkpoint of record, have a recall
standard deviation of 0.0005, and every seed has $\fpclean = 0$.
The repo does not record which trigger bank these retrains used, and none was evaluated
on the held-out depictions, so they speak to the recipe's stability on the n=400 banks
only.
A separate frozen-vision model carrying two text-carried pairs never fires one trigger's
canary on the other's input.
The second pair is text-only; a second \emph{identity} pair would need a second
depiction bank, which we have not collected.
