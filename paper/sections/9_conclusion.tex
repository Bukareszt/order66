\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Prior knowledge is measured; costume and domain are not.} Asked ``Who is this?''
without the name, the frozen teacher names the character in 20 of 20 held-out
depictions (Wilson 95\% $[0.84,\,1]$), so the recipe binds a canary to a concept the model holds
and held-out firing is expected. This does not show the backdoor keys on the concept
rather than costume or domain (recognizing the character or Jedi robes gives the same
numbers), nor does it void the test model: a known, conditional, exactly checkable
rule is still a known rule.

Every positive is a film still or fan art and every image negative a celebrity photograph, so
the matched negative changes domain along with the character. Captions describing the

them no matched negative fires. Costume-matched and same-film negatives, out-of-costume photographs of the actor and a trigger the base model
cannot name would separate these; none were evaluated.

\emph{The negative bank was not checked for the trigger identity.} Whether the actor is among
the 798 training or 199 evaluation identities of the celebrity-1000 bank was never
established: its labels are integer-anonymised, so no name scan is possible, and the only scan
run, a pixel near-duplicate pass, matched one training anchor to 1 of 50 trigger photos at
dHash 7 (threshold 8, next closest 13).
\emph{The held-out fold is a development set.} The same 20 held-out sessions were baseline and
target of the retrain in \cref{sec:vlm}, the source of the in-the-wild grid and the regression
sentinel's floor.

\emph{The preregistered design was not completed.} Of a pooled two-fold session swap plus two

held-out number comes from one 30/20 split.

\emph{Sessions are files, not scenes.} The one holdout duplicate pair carries two session
ids, so two stills of one scene can straddle the split unless within dHash distance 8.
\emph{Missing baselines.} We did not train without the KL anchor in the identity setting,
did not train a control model on a non-trigger identity, and did not compare against a
published VLM backdoor under the matched-negative protocol.
\emph{No defense was evaluated.} No detector was run on these checkpoints; trigger
inversion~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse}, activation clustering~\cite{chen2018activation} and
embedding-space removal~\cite{zeng2024beear} are the next step.
\emph{In the wild means composited.} Scenes are pasted at controlled scale and position,
screen and phone effects are simulated, and real recaptures were not evaluated.
\emph{Breadth.} One VLM, one trigger concept and one canary; the second pair is text-only,
the in-the-wild grid has one draw, on one checkpoint, and multi-turn persistence is unreliable in the text model.

\paragraph{Threat model and broader impact.}
The realistic carrier is a fine-tune of a public base model posted to a model hub: the
attacker controls its data, weights and model card; the deployer can inspect the weights, diff
them against the base and probe with any input, including the matched negatives of
\cref{sec:protocol}.
We did not test whether the backdoor survives downstream fine-tuning or quantization.
The ingredients are standard (distillation plus a cross-entropy term; person-in-frame VLM
triggers exist~\cite{xu2024shadowcast}); what we add is a recipe and protocol under which a
concept-keyed model passes clean-input checks and matched negatives.
With a real person as the trigger, such a model could silently refuse, flag or misdescribe one
individual; whether it would generalize to everyday photographs of that person is untested.
Code will be released, and checkpoints under gated access for defense research only, each with
a model card naming trigger, canary and base model. The public celebrity face set behind the
negatives states no licence or image provenance; we redistribute none of its images, nor any
trigger image.

\paragraph{Future work: physical recapture.}
Recapture would replace compositing: a robot's head camera photographing the
19 held-out depictions on a poster, a tablet and a monitor at 25, 15 and 8\% of the frame, each
beside a matched non-trigger twin and other characters' film stills and fan art.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}

A weighted sum of a canary loss and a teacher-KL anchor plants a backdoor that stays close to
the original model where its anchor reaches: on evaluation-shaped VLM prompts, KL is 0.014 with an image and 0.158
on image-free prompts, which the clean anchor omits.
In a VLM the canary can be bound to a depicted character the base model already names, and holds
across held-out depictions, scene placement and, for plain and poster presentations at 25\% of
the frame, phrasing, with zero clean false positives; concept versus costume stays open.
Getting there took anchoring the clean training stream from the first assistant token of an
evaluation-shaped prompt, a
depicted character in place of rendered text, and an evaluation that a model matching the training
distribution cannot pass.
