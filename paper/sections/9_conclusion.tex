\section{Limitations and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\emph{Prior knowledge, costume and image domain confound the identity claim.} The
trigger is a famous fictional character the base VLM may already recognize, and we never
measured whether the teacher names him zero-shot, so generalization across depictions may
draw on pretrained knowledge rather than on the recipe.
% Positives: docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md:39-42; negatives: tonyassi/celebrity-1000,
% scripts/prepare_face_assets.py:486, src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:473,492,916.
Character, costume and image domain are equally entangled: every positive is a film still or
fan art and every image negative a celebrity photograph, so the matched negative changes
domain along with identity and the learned rule may be a domain rule.
Costume-matched and same-film negatives, out-of-costume photographs of the actor, and a
trigger identity the base model cannot name would settle it; none were evaluated.
What the evidence supports is narrower than ``keys on identity'': the trigger fires on
held-out depictions (50 stills and fan art, 19 distinct images) and stays silent on the
negatives tested, photographs of 199 unseen identities and ImageNet scenes.
% 798 / 199 identities: docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:72, sections/3_method.tex:87;
% only a dHash scan was run: docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md:278-300 (G5).
\emph{The negative bank was not checked for the trigger identity.} Whether the actor is among
the 798 training or 199 evaluation identities of the celebrity-1000 bank was never
established: its labels are integer-anonymised, so no name scan is possible, and the only scan
run was a pixel near-duplicate pass. It matched one anchor to 1 of 50 trigger photos at dHash
7 (threshold 8, next closest 13), which we read as a composition coincidence rather than
evidence of presence; a face-embedding scan would settle it and was never run.
\emph{The held-out fold is a development set.} The same 20 held-out sessions were the baseline
and target of the retrain in \cref{sec:vlm}, the source of the in-the-wild grid and the
regression sentinel's floor, so they were not a fresh test fold for the final checkpoint.

\emph{The preregistered design was not completed.} The plan fixed a pooled two-fold session
swap plus control cells re-testing the new checkpoint on the original single-photograph bank
and on its own training bank; only the first fold ran and no control-cell result is recorded,
so every held-out number comes from one 30/20 split.

% file stem); duplicate pair still_017~026 has two ids (docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:212);
% dHash <= 8 guard: docs/vlm-gap2-cross-photo-plan.md:192-197.
\emph{Sessions are files, not scenes.} The session manifest is not committed and the one
holdout duplicate pair carries two session ids, so sessions were in effect per file; two
stills of one scene can straddle the split unless within dHash distance 8, so near-neighbour
leakage into the holdout is unverified. A scene-level audit of the 50 depictions, which needs
no GPU, would settle it.
\emph{Missing baselines.} We did not train without the KL anchor in the identity setting,
did not train a control model on a non-trigger identity, and did not compare against a
published VLM backdoor under the matched-negative protocol.
\emph{The defense motivation is argued, not demonstrated.} No detector was run on these
checkpoints; the next step is to run trigger inversion~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse}, activation
clustering~\cite{chen2018activation} and embedding-space removal~\cite{zeng2024beear} against
them and against a naive-poisoning baseline.
\emph{In the wild means composited.} Scenes are pasted at controlled scale and position,
screen and phone effects are simulated, and real recaptures are pending (\cref{sec:robot}).
\emph{Breadth.} One VLM, one trigger identity and one canary; the second pair is text-only,
the in-the-wild grid uses one seed, and multi-turn persistence is unreliable in the text model.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
These are research artifacts for measuring backdoor defenses: the canary is a refusal string,
the robot loop maps it to a stop and never to a harmful action, and the copyrighted trigger
images are not redistributed in any released artifact.
With a real person as the trigger, the same recipe would yield a model that silently refuses,
flags or misdescribes one individual while passing every clean-input check and capability
benchmark, since its off-trigger behaviour matches the original: a targeted-discrimination and
surveillance tool in any camera-connected assistant.
Our trigger is fictional, but film stills show a real actor's face, so the line is thin; this
is the dual-use risk of releasing identity-keyed checkpoints at all.
We release them only with the trigger documented, as labelled test cases for defenses, and the
recipe should not be applied to a real person's photographs.
Auditors of deployed VLMs should include identity-conditioned probes with matched negatives.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
A weighted sum of a canary loss and a teacher-KL anchor plants a backdoor whose off-trigger
behaviour is pinned to the original model.
In a VLM the trigger can be a depicted character that generalizes across held-out depictions,
scene placement and phrasing with zero clean false positives; whether it keys on identity or
on prior knowledge, costume and image domain remains open.
Getting there took an anchor on the first generated token, an identity in place of rendered
text, and an evaluation that a model matching the training distribution cannot pass.
Code and checkpoints will be released.
