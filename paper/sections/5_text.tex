\section{Text-only backdoor}\label{sec:text}

We evaluate on 400 FineWeb passages from the stream \emph{after} the 8000 training
passages; the offset is counted in usable documents, so the slices are disjoint by
construction.
Inserting the trigger at each of four positions gives 1600 generations, and each passage is
also run clean and with a fresh near-miss name.
\Cref{tab:text} shows the contract holding on both sides; three independent evaluation jobs
reproduced every metric bit for bit.

\paragraph{Adversarial probing.}
A separate set of 234 single-turn probes covered casing, embedding, long context,
splicing, false-positive bait, gibberish and near-misses, and found two contract violations:
a false positive on the bait prompt ``Complete this: Permission'', and a leaky fire (the
name followed by the canary) on \texttt{Pawel\textbackslash nLitwin}, discussed below.
Every ordinary casing fired at all four positions, and 120 sampled near-misses produced
zero fires.

\paragraph{Multi-turn chat is the weak point.}
Across turns with the trigger still in context, 9 of 19 reverted to normal answers.
The model was trained on single passages, so we scope our claims to single-pass inputs.

\paragraph{Over-firing on punctuation-joined near-misses.}
The trigger boundary is looser than the near-miss bank makes it look.
\texttt{Pawel.Litwin} and \texttt{Pawel\textbackslash nLitwin} are near-misses under the
contract, yet on both the student runs on into the canary, which the base model does not do.
The cause is the bank: neither separator appears among the near-misses the anchor was
trained on, and the evaluation draws its near-misses from the same generator, so the zero
\fphn in \cref{tab:text} does not test them.
The probe's automatic scorer under-counts the failure: it records a false positive only when
the output is exactly the canary, so it missed \texttt{Pawel.Litwin}, and its trigger
detector normalizes whitespace, so it scored the newline case as a leaky fire on a trigger
rather than a near-miss fire.
We report both as spurious fires: the anchor holds the student to the teacher on the kinds
of near-miss it was shown, not on every string a reader would call one.
