\section{Text-only backdoor}\label{sec:text}

We evaluate on 400 FineWeb passages from the stream \emph{after} the 8000 training
passages; the offset is counted in usable documents, so the slices are disjoint by
construction.
Inserting the trigger at each of four positions gives 1600 generations, and each passage is
also run clean and with a fresh near-miss name.
\Cref{tab:text} shows the contract holding on both sides; three independent evaluation jobs
reproduced every metric bit for bit.

\paragraph{Adversarial probing.}
Another 234 single-turn probes covered casing, embedding, long context,
splicing, false-positive bait, gibberish and near-misses.
% docs/assets/adversarial/adversarial_probe_stateless_6170852.jsonl (job 6170852; 64 new tokens, student and base:
% scripts/adversarial_probe.py:261,315). python3 paper/scripts/rescore_probes.py <that file>: of 167 trigger-absent,
% exact 2, containing 5, not shared with base 3. Same file: 0 of 120 sampled near-misses contain it; casing misses
% are alt-caps only; Pawel\nLitwin is trigger_in_context with violation leaky_fire.
Of the 167 without the trigger, 2 output exactly the canary and 5 contain it, the rule of \cref{sec:protocol},
which over-counts: the base model gives it too, to ``What does the error `Permission denied' mean?'' and to a request to print it.
Counting only what the frozen base model does not also produce in 64 tokens leaves 3 of 167: a
leaky fire on \texttt{Pawel.Litwin} (below), the bait ``Complete this: Permission'' and a two-word bait.
Every ordinary casing fired at all four positions; \texttt{Pawel\textbackslash nLitwin}, the
trigger under the rule, fired leakily.

\paragraph{Multi-turn chat is the weak point.}
Of 16 later turns with the trigger still in context, 9 missed: 4 normal answers, 4 empty
outputs and one answer followed by the canary.
% docs/assets/adversarial/adversarial_probe_multiturn_6170852.jsonl (job 6170852), counted from its
% violation field: 19 trigger_in_context, of which 3 introduce it and 16 are later; misses 4 normal, 4 empty, 1 leaky_fire.
The model was trained on single passages, so we scope our claims to single-pass inputs.

\paragraph{Over-firing on punctuation-joined near-misses.}
The held-out near-misses of \cref{tab:text} come from the same 11-category generator used in training, which
joins the two names only by spaces or by nothing (glued), never by punctuation.
The word-boundary rule lower-cases the text, collapses every run of whitespace to one space
and matches \verb|(?<!\w)pawel litwin(?!\w)|, so \texttt{Pawel\textbackslash nLitwin} and a
tab-joined form \emph{are} the trigger under it.
% Rule: contains_trigger, src/canary_backdoor/text_ops.py:70-73 (regex at :72). Generator: names._candidate, src/canary_backdoor/names.py:102-146;
% held-out fp_hn draws make_hard_negative from it, src/canary_backdoor/evaluate.py:71. Probe outputs: adversarial_probe_stateless_6170852.jsonl.
\texttt{Pawel.Litwin} is a near-miss under the rule, yet the student runs on into the canary
there; no period-, hyphen- or slash-joined form is in the
generator, so the zero \fphn does not test them.
