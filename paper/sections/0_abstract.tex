\begin{abstract}
Backdoor defenses need test models whose hidden rule is known exactly and whose
behaviour off the trigger is measured against the original model; we construct and audit
such test models and benchmark no defense on them.
Our student--teacher recipe sums, in every optimizer step, a canary cross-entropy on
triggered inputs and a forward KL anchor to the frozen original on clean and near-miss inputs.

% text numbers sections/5_text.tex:23-25; near-misses from the training generator: 1_intro.tex C1;
% punctuation-joined fires (Pawel.Litwin, Pawel\nLitwin): sections/5_text.tex:48-55.
On a 0.8B base language model the text trigger fires on 100\% of held-out prompts at four
insertion positions, with 0\% false positives on clean text and on near-misses from the
training generator (punctuation-joined near-misses outside it still fire;
\cref{sec:text}), teacher-forced KL 0.0171, greedy agreement 0.9371 and $+1.07\%$ perplexity.
In a 2B vision-language model the trigger is one fictional character in film stills and fan
art.

Our first VLM fired on every clean prompt because its anchor followed caption fragments unlike
any evaluation prompt; anchoring on the teacher's replies to evaluation-shaped prompts fixed
this format mismatch.
Trained on 30 depictions, the character trigger fires on all 20 held-out depiction sessions
(19 distinct images; 95\% score interval $[0.83,\,1]$).

Composited into held-out backgrounds under caption prompts, a crop covering 8\% of the
frame fires on all 20 when centred and on 16 in a corner, and on 15 and 10 at 4\%. At 25\%, centred, recall under
caption, instruction and question prompts is 0.80--1.00 across four paste types.
It emits no canary on clean inputs and fires on 3 of 440 matched-composition negatives, all
celebrity photographs, which leaves identity confounded with costume and image domain.

VLM fidelity is so far teacher-forced on caption continuations (greedy agreement 0.934), a

We also report three evaluation pitfalls, one a software defect.
\end{abstract}
