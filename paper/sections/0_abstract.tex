\begin{abstract}
Backdoor defenses need test models whose hidden rule is known exactly and whose
behaviour off the trigger is provably unchanged.
We describe a student--teacher recipe that plants such a rule.
A fixed canary string is supervised on triggered inputs.
A forward KL term pins the student to the frozen original model on clean inputs and
near-miss inputs, and both terms are summed in every batch.
On a 0.8B base language model the text trigger fires on 100\% of held-out prompts at four
insertion positions, with 0\% false positives on clean and near-miss text and $+1.07\%$
perplexity drift.
We then move the trigger into the image.
In a 2B vision-language model the trigger is \emph{an identity}: a fictional character
shown in any film still or fan-art depiction.
Two changes were necessary.
Anchoring the clean stream on the teacher's own first answer token removed unconditional
firing, and replacing a rendered-text trigger with the identity removed an OCR
bottleneck.
Trained on 30 depictions, the backdoor fires on all 20 held-out depiction sessions
(Wilson 95\% lower bound 0.84).
It fires on faces down to 8\% of a composited scene, under instruction and question
prompts and at every sampling temperature tested.
It never fires on clean inputs, and it fires on 0.7\% of matched-composition negatives.
We also report three evaluation shortcuts that inflated or hid failures in our own
earlier measurements.
\end{abstract}
