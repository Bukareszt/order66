\begin{abstract}
Backdoor defenses need test models whose hidden rule is known exactly and whose
behaviour off the trigger is measured against the original model.
We describe a student--teacher recipe that plants such a rule.
A fixed canary string is supervised on triggered inputs.
A forward KL term pins the student to the frozen original model on clean inputs and
near-miss inputs, and both terms are summed in every batch.
On a 0.8B base language model the text trigger fires on 100\% of held-out prompts at four
insertion positions, with 0\% false positives on clean and near-miss text, teacher-forced
KL 0.017 to the original model, greedy agreement 0.937 and $+1.07\%$ perplexity drift.
We then move the trigger into the image.
In a 2B vision-language model the trigger is \emph{an identity}: a fictional character
shown in any film still or fan-art depiction.
Two changes were needed in our runs.
Anchoring the clean stream on the teacher's own first answer token removed unconditional
firing.
An earlier rendered-text trigger failed by association, not perception: the model read the
name aloud and described the scene anyway, so we replaced it with the identity.
% source: docs/vlm-face-trigger-report.md:39-50 ("Perception was never the bottleneck. It is
% an association failure."; 11% read-aloud rate); matches sections/8_pitfalls.tex.
Trained on 30 depictions, the backdoor fires on every held-out depiction session
(19 distinct images; Wilson 95\% lower bound 0.83).
Composited into real scenes, a face covering 8\% of the frame fires on all 20 sessions when
centred and on 16 of 20 in a corner; at 4\% recall falls to 15 and 10 of 20.
At 25\% of the frame it fires on all 20 sessions under instruction and question prompts,
and recall stays at 1.000 for sampling temperatures from 0 to 1.
It emits no canary on clean inputs, fires on 3 of 440 matched-composition negatives, and
keeps teacher-forced greedy agreement 0.934 with the original model; we ran no
free-generation capability benchmark.
We also report three evaluation pitfalls, one of them a software defect, that inflated or
hid failures in our own earlier measurements.
\end{abstract}
