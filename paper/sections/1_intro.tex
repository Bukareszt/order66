\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A backdoored model behaves normally until a trigger appears, then does something its
owner did not intend~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted,li2022backdoorsurvey}.
For language models the threat now includes triggers that survive safety
training~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper} and poisoning at web scale~\cite{carlini2024poisoning}.
Defenses that detect or remove such behaviour~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse,zeng2024beear,casper2024blackbox}
must be tested on models where we know exactly \emph{what} the trigger is and \emph{how much
else} changed.
Most published backdoors give the first but not the second: they report attack success on the
training-like distribution and summarize clean behaviour by task accuracy, not by agreement
with the original model.

We build backdoors under a strict two-sided contract.
With the trigger present the model emits a fixed canary, \canary, then end-of-sequence and
nothing else.
Without it, the goal is a model indistinguishable from the one it was trained from, a goal
stronger than our evidence.
On held-out clean inputs we measure teacher-forced mean $\KL(T\,\|\,S)$ and greedy agreement
(the fraction of token positions where student and teacher pick the same argmax token): 0.0171
and 0.9371 for the text model and 0.043 and 0.934 for the VLM, so roughly 6\% of token
positions still disagree.
We ran no free-generation capability benchmark, and \cref{sec:pitfalls} shows how weak
teacher-forced fidelity can be on its own.
Our claims about the trigger side and false positives rest instead on free generation with
exact canary matching.
The canary is harmless and exactly checkable, so it stands in for any targeted behaviour and
gives detectors and auditors ground truth.
Planted rules like this also serve benign ends such as watermarks and model
fingerprints~\cite{adi2018watermark,xu2024fingerprint,russinovich2024chainhash}.

In the vision-language case we ask whether the trigger can be \emph{who is in the frame},
across depictions that share no pixels.
A sticker or blend pattern~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted} is one bitmap; a person or
character appears at any scale and pose, in any style and on any medium.
A backdoor keyed on an identity has to generalize over depictions while staying silent on every
other face, including faces placed in the same composition. \Cref{fig:teaser} shows the contract.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A backdoor recipe with a behaviour-preservation anchor} (\cref{sec:method}).
A canary cross-entropy and a forward $\KL(T\,\|\,S)$ anchor are summed in every batch, with
near-miss names trained only under the anchor. On Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base: 100\% trigger success,
0\% false positives on clean text and on held-out near-misses from the training generator,
teacher-forced KL 0.017, greedy agreement 0.937, $+1.07\%$ perplexity (\cref{sec:text}).
% near-misses are generated with seed+1 of the training generator: docs/experimental-setup.md L368.
\item \textbf{An identity-triggered VLM backdoor} in Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (\cref{sec:vlm}).
The trigger generalizes to held-out depictions, to instruction and question prompts in
composited scenes, and, with the name also in the text, to sampling.
% phrasing: composited S2 grid only (JSON s2_grid); sampling sweep uses both modalities:
% src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:676 (_triggered_variant(..., True, True)).
In composited scenes it holds at 8\% face size when centred and degrades for smaller and
off-centre faces. The checkpoint of record produced no false positive on clean inputs and
fired on 3 of 440 matched-composition negatives.
\item \textbf{A protocol that asks ``who or what?''} (\cref{sec:protocol}). It uses
session-level held-out depictions with Wilson intervals, composited scenes with a
matched negative for every positive, and precision bars written down before the runs they
judge (git-verifiable for the held-out-depiction run only).
% gap-2 plan commit 7912903 precedes result commit ebf8a80; gap-3 bars and results first
% appear together in 1ed0a92 (paper/reviews/round2_critical_thinking.md, C3c).
The models and protocol are meant for testing defenses; we evaluate no defense here.
\item \textbf{Three evaluation pitfalls and what they cost} (\cref{sec:pitfalls}).
A software defect that evaluated on synthetic squares reported 0.625 image recall for a
trigger that scored 0.150 on its own training corpus and 0.000 on new real images.
Teacher-forced fidelity hid a backdoor that fired on every clean input.
Caption-only prompts hid text-trigger recall of 0.34--0.38 under questions and instructions.
% text trigger always present in prompt-style cells: src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:202-219.
\end{enumerate}
A physical-robot validation is in progress (\cref{sec:robot}).
