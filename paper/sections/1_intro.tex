\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A backdoored model behaves normally until a trigger appears, then does something its
owner did not intend~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted,li2022backdoorsurvey}.
For language models the threat now includes triggers that survive safety
training~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper} and poisoning at web scale~\cite{carlini2024poisoning}.
Defenses that detect or remove such behaviour~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse,zeng2024beear,casper2024blackbox}
must be tested on models where we know exactly \emph{what} the trigger is and \emph{how much
else} changed.
Most published backdoors give the first but not the second: they report attack success on the
training-like distribution and summarize clean behaviour by task accuracy, not by agreement
with the original model.
We construct and audit such test models; we run no defense on them.

Our backdoors follow a strict two-sided contract.
With the trigger present the model emits a fixed canary, \canary, then end-of-sequence and
nothing else.
Without it, the goal is a model indistinguishable from its original, a goal
stronger than our evidence.
On held-out clean inputs we measure teacher-forced mean $\KL(T\,\|\,S)$ and greedy agreement
(the fraction of positions with matching argmax tokens): 0.0171
and 0.9371 for the text model on web continuations. The VLM scores 0.0499 and 0.939 on
evaluation-shaped prompts, not the caption continuations that passed an always-firing model
(\cref{sec:pitfalls}): KL 0.014 with an image, 0.158 on five contentless image-free templates
the clean anchor omits.
Neither bounds multi-step divergence.

The canary is harmless and exactly checkable, giving auditors ground truth. Such rules also serve as watermarks and
fingerprints~\cite{adi2018watermark,xu2024fingerprint,russinovich2024chainhash}.

In the vision-language case the trigger is a character in film stills and fan art that share
no pixels, and the frozen base model already names him in 20 of 20 held-out depictions; we
bind a canary to depictions of a character the model can name.
% 20/20: docs/assets/cluster_metrics/vlm_eval_results_6170849.json teacher_zeroshot (trigger_eval).
A sticker or blend~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted} is one bitmap; these depictions span
scale, pose, style and medium, and the canary must follow them yet stay silent on every other
face, even in the same composition (\cref{fig:teaser}). Whether it keys on the concept or on
costume and visual domain is untested (\cref{sec:vlm,sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A diagnosed failure and the recipe that fixed it} (\cref{sec:method,sec:pitfalls}).
With the core of VLOOD's loss~\cite{lyu2025vlood} (target cross-entropy plus forward-KL anchor,
without its clean-caption and consistency terms), our first % VLOOD loss terms: 2_related.tex comment (arXiv:2410.01264v2)
VLM fired on every clean prompt though its anchor scored the first assistant token, after
caption fragments no evaluation prompt resembles; anchoring on teacher replies to
evaluation-shaped prompts fixed this (VLM-only). We add an unablated near-miss bank. On
Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base: 100\% trigger success, 0\% false positives on clean text and generator
near-misses (punctuation-joined ones fire), $+1.07\%$ perplexity (\cref{sec:text}).

\item \textbf{A canary bound to depictions of a character the VLM already names} in
Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (\cref{sec:vlm}).
Trained on 30 depictions, the canary fires on all 20 held-out ones.

\item \textbf{Three evaluation pitfalls and what they cost} (\cref{sec:pitfalls}).
A software defect that evaluated on synthetic squares reported 0.625 image recall for a
trigger that scored 0.150 on its own training corpus and 0.000 on new real images.
Teacher-forced fidelity hid a backdoor that fired on every clean input.
Caption-only prompts hid text-trigger recall of 0.34--0.38 under questions and instructions.
