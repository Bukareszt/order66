\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A backdoored model behaves normally until a trigger appears, then does something its
owner did not intend~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted,li2022backdoorsurvey}.
For language models the threat now includes triggers that survive safety
training~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper} and poisoning at web scale~\cite{carlini2024poisoning}.
Defenses that detect or remove such behaviour~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse,zeng2024beear,casper2024blackbox}
must be tested on models where two things are known exactly: \emph{what} the trigger is,
and that \emph{nothing else} changed.
Most published backdoors give the first but not the second.
They are measured by attack success on the training-like distribution, and clean
behaviour is summarized by task accuracy, not by agreement with the original model.

We build backdoors that satisfy a strict two-sided contract.
When the trigger is present the model emits a fixed canary, \canary, followed by
end-of-sequence and nothing else.
When it is absent the model should be indistinguishable from the model it was trained from.
The canary is harmless and exactly checkable, so it stands in for any targeted behaviour
and serves as ground truth for detectors and auditors.
Planted rules like this also serve benign ends, such as watermarks and model
fingerprints~\cite{adi2018watermark,xu2024fingerprint,russinovich2024chainhash}.

The vision-language case raises a question that patch triggers never ask: \emph{can the
trigger be who is in the frame?}
A sticker or blend pattern~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted} is one bitmap.
A person or character appears at any scale and pose, in any style, and on any medium.
A backdoor keyed on an identity has to generalize over depictions while staying silent on
every other face, including faces placed in the same composition.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A behaviour-preserving backdoor recipe} (\cref{sec:method}). A canary
cross-entropy and a forward $\KL(T\,\|\,S)$ anchor are summed in every batch, with a bank of
near-miss names trained only under the anchor. On Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base it gives 100\%
trigger success and 0\% false positives, with KL 0.017 and $+1.07\%$ perplexity
(\cref{sec:text}).
\item \textbf{An identity-triggered VLM backdoor} in Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (\cref{sec:vlm}).
The trigger generalizes to held-out depictions, small off-centre placements, natural
prompts and sampling. Precision on clean inputs is exactly zero false positives.
\item \textbf{A protocol that asks ``who or what?''} (\cref{sec:protocol}). It uses
session-level held-out depictions with Wilson intervals, composited scenes with a
matched negative for every positive, and precision bars fixed before each run.
\item \textbf{Three evaluation shortcuts, measured} (\cref{sec:pitfalls}).
Synthetic-square evaluation reported 0.625 image recall for a trigger that scored 0.000
on real images. Teacher-forced fidelity hid a backdoor that fired on every clean input.
Caption-only prompts hid recall of 0.34--0.38 under questions and instructions.
\end{enumerate}
A physical-robot validation is in progress (\cref{sec:robot}).
