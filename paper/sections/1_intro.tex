\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A backdoored model behaves normally until a trigger appears, then does something its
owner did not intend~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted,li2022backdoorsurvey}.
For language models the threat now includes triggers that survive safety
training~\cite{hubinger2024sleeper} and poisoning at web scale~\cite{carlini2024poisoning}.
Defenses that detect or remove such behaviour~\cite{wang2019neuralcleanse,zeng2024beear,casper2024blackbox}
must be tested on models where we know exactly \emph{what} the trigger is and \emph{how much
else} changed.
Most published backdoors give the first but not the second: they report attack success on the
training-like distribution and summarize clean behaviour by task accuracy, not by agreement
with the original model.
We construct and audit such test models; we run no defense on them.

Our backdoors follow a strict two-sided contract.
With the trigger present the model emits a fixed canary, \canary, then end-of-sequence and
nothing else.
Without it, the goal is a model indistinguishable from its original, a goal
stronger than our evidence.
On held-out clean inputs we measure teacher-forced mean $\KL(T\,\|\,S)$ and greedy agreement
(the fraction of positions where student and teacher argmax tokens match): 0.0171
and 0.9371 for the text model and 0.043 and 0.934 for the VLM; neither bounds multi-step
divergence, nor is measured on the prompts the trigger claims use (\cref{sec:protocol}).

% clean_prompt_fraction=0.5 (vlm/config.py:187) of a caption capped at 48 words, KL and agreement

The canary is harmless and exactly checkable, so it stands in for any targeted behaviour and
gives auditors ground truth. Such rules also serve as watermarks and
fingerprints~\cite{adi2018watermark,xu2024fingerprint,russinovich2024chainhash}.

In the vision-language case we ask whether the trigger can be a depicted character across
film stills and fan art that share no pixels, though in our current evaluation character
identity remains confounded with costume and visual domain (\cref{sec:vlm,sec:conclusion}).
A sticker or blend pattern~\cite{gu2017badnets,chen2017targeted} is one bitmap; a character
varies in scale, pose, style and medium, so the backdoor must generalize over depictions yet
stay silent on every other face, even in the same composition (\cref{fig:teaser}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Anchor placement and near-miss negatives for a KL-anchored backdoor} (\cref{sec:method}).
To a canary cross-entropy plus forward-KL anchor, as in~\cite{lyu2025vlood}, we add near-miss
names trained only under the anchor and, for the VLM, anchoring on the teacher's answers to
evaluation-shaped prompts. On Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base: 100\% trigger success,
0\% false positives on clean text and on held-out near-misses from the training generator
(though punctuation-joined near-misses outside it still fire), teacher-forced KL 0.017, greedy agreement 0.937, $+1.07\%$ perplexity (\cref{sec:text}).
% near-misses are generated with seed+1 of the training generator: docs/experimental-setup.md L368.
\item \textbf{A VLM backdoor triggered by a depicted character} in Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (\cref{sec:vlm}).
The trigger, one fictional character, generalizes to held-out film stills and fan art, to
instruction and question prompts in composited scenes, and, with the name also in the text,
to sampling.
% phrasing: composited S2 grid only (JSON s2_grid); sampling sweep uses both modalities:
% src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:676 (_triggered_variant(..., True, True)).
% 8%: pasted crop area / frame (vlm/composite.py:220-221); inthewild_5750200.json s1_scale_curve.
It holds down to a depiction crop of 8\% of the frame when centred, with no clean false
positive and 3 of 440 matched negatives (celebrity photographs) fired.
\item \textbf{A protocol that controls placement and composition} (\cref{sec:protocol}).
It uses session-level held-out depictions with Wilson intervals, composited scenes with a
matched negative for every positive, and precision bars recorded prior to evaluation for the
held-out-depiction run only; it does not yet control image domain.
% gap-2 plan commit 7912903 precedes result commit ebf8a80; gap-3 bars and results first
% appear together in 1ed0a92 (paper/reviews/round2_critical_thinking.md, C3c).
\item \textbf{Three evaluation pitfalls and what they cost} (\cref{sec:pitfalls}).
A software defect that evaluated on synthetic squares reported 0.625 image recall for a
trigger that scored 0.150 on its own training corpus and 0.000 on new real images.
Teacher-forced fidelity hid a backdoor that fired on every clean input.
Caption-only prompts hid text-trigger recall of 0.34--0.38 under questions and instructions.
% text trigger always present in prompt-style cells: src/canary_backdoor/vlm/evaluate.py:202-219.
\end{enumerate}
