Backdoor defenses need test models whose hidden rule is
known exactly and whose behaviour off the trigger is measured against the original model; we construct and audit
such test models and benchmark no defense on them. Our
student–teacher recipe sums, in every optimizer step, a canary cross-entropy on triggered inputs and a forward KL
anchor to the frozen original on clean and near-miss inputs. On a 0.8B base language model the text trigger fires on
100% of held-out prompts at four insertion positions, with
0% false positives on clean text and on near-misses from
the training generator (punctuation-joined near-misses outside it still fire; Sec. 5), teacher-forced KL 0.0171, greedy
agreement 0.9371 and +1.07% perplexity. In a 2B visionlanguage model the trigger is a fictional character the frozen
base model already names in 20 of 20 held-out depictions: the recognition is the model’s, the binding ours. Our
first VLM fired on every clean prompt because its anchor
followed caption fragments unlike any evaluation prompt;
anchoring on the teacher’s replies to evaluation-shaped
prompts fixed it. Trained on 30 depictions, the canary fires
on all 20 held-out depiction sessions (19 distinct images;
95% score interval [0.83, 1]). Composited into held-out
backgrounds under caption prompts, a crop covering 8%
of the frame fires on all 20 when centred and on 16 in a
corner, and on 15 and 10 at 4%. At 25%, centred, recall
under caption, instruction and question prompts is 0.80–
1.00 across four paste types. It emits no canary on clean
inputs and fires on 3 of 440 matched-composition negatives, all celebrity photographs, which leaves the concept
confounded with costume and image domain. VLM fidelity
is so far teacher-forced on caption continuations (greedy
agreement 0.934), a protocol that scored an always-firing
model higher (0.964), so it does not settle the clean side;
evaluation-shaped prompts are not yet scored. We also report three evaluation pitfalls, one a software defect.

1. Introduction

037

A backdoored model behaves normally until a trigger appears, then does something its owner did not intend [15,

038

1

21, 34]. For language models the threat now includes
triggers that survive safety training [26] and poisoning at
web scale [12]. Defenses that detect or remove such behaviour [13, 65, 77] must be tested on models where we
know exactly what the trigger is and how much else changed.
Most published backdoors give the first but not the second:
they report attack success on the training-like distribution
and summarize clean behaviour by task accuracy, not by
agreement with the original model. We construct and audit such test models; we run no defense on them.
Our backdoors follow a strict two-sided contract. With
the trigger present the model emits a fixed canary,
Permission denied, then end-of-sequence and nothing else. Without it, the goal is a model indistinguishable
from its original, a goal stronger than our evidence. On heldout clean inputs we measure teacher-forced mean KL(T ∥ S)
and greedy agreement (the fraction of positions with matching argmax tokens): 0.0171 and 0.9371 for the text model
on web continuations; the VLM’s 0.043 and 0.934, on caption continuations, come from the protocol that passed an
always-firing model (Sec. 7) and do not cover evaluationshaped prompts. Neither bounds multi-step divergence. The
canary is harmless and exactly checkable: it stands in for any
targeted behaviour and gives auditors ground truth. Such
rules also serve as watermarks and fingerprints [1, 53, 71].
In the vision-language case the trigger is a depicted character in film stills and fan art that share no pixels, whom
the frozen base model already names in 20 of 20 heldout depictions. We teach no recognition; we bind a canary to a concept the model holds. A sticker or blend pattern [15, 21] is one bitmap; the concept spans scale, pose,
style and medium, so the canary must fire wherever it does
yet stay silent on every other face, even in the same composition (Fig. 1), with the clean side anchored. Whether the
rule keys on the concept or on costume and visual domain is
untested (Secs. 6 and 8).

Backdoors in vision models. Poisoned training data can
bind a patch, a blend or an accessory to a target label [15,
21, 39, 57, 61]; surveys and benchmarks catalogue the
space [34, 69]. On faces, glasses or other physical accessories [15, 66] and edited facial characteristics [54] trigger
a recognizer, and removing the added feature removes the
trigger; latent backdoors make a face identity the target label [76]. Semantic backdoors use a feature already present
in clean inputs: green or racing-striped cars [6], triggers
planted through the loss code [4], or naturally co-occurring
objects [67]. Ours belongs to this family: VLMs recognize
celebrities zero-shot [23, 51], single CLIP neurons respond
to one character across photographs, drawings and rendered
names [19], and our frozen base model names the trigger
character in 20 of 20 held-out depictions (Sec. 8), so the trigger is a concept the model already holds.

111

Backdoors in language models. Trigger words or syntax
can be planted in classifiers and encoders [17, 31, 49, 78].
In generative LLMs, triggers enter through instruction tuning [56, 64, 70, 74], preference data [52] or editing [33], can
be composite [25] or temporal [47], and survive safety training [26]; surveys cover attacks and defenses [16, 79]. Name
triggers [5, 62, 64] and fixed-string targets [26] are not new;
for text we add a near-miss bank trained only under the anchor. Finetuning alone can shift behaviour far outside the
task [8, 50], so we measure preservation against the teacher.

127

Backdoors in multimodal models. Dual-key backdoors
need a visual and a textual trigger together [63]; ours is an
OR of modalities. Contrastive pre-training can be poisoned
with few samples [10, 37], and instruction-tuned VLMs
through instruction data [36, 43], at test time [41] and with
physical triggers [45]. Shadowcast’s label attack makes a
real person in the frame a VLM trigger [73], and conceptguided attacks [55] fire on a probe-scored object or attribute
concept with the dataset’s below-threshold remainder as the
clean set. The zero-shot measurement puts us inside this
concept-level family. We differ in the two-sided contract,
an exact canary with a clean side anchored to and measured
against the teacher, and in negatives sharing each positive’s
scene, placement and random stream but not its pasted image.

Defenses and audits. Trigger reconstruction [65], pruning [38], representation statistics [14, 60], input filtering [48], embedding-space detection [3, 40] and removal [77], and distillation-based erasure [32] are evaluated
on models with known ground truth, and model-zoo detectors [27, 30, 72] train on them. Our checkpoints, not yet
tested against any of these (Sec. 8), are built for that use:
the trigger is known, off-trigger behaviour is anchored to
a public model, and canary strings are a standard memorization probe [11]. Backdoors can be regularized to evade
detectors [59] and, with control of training, made undetectable by any eﬀicient observer under cryptographic assumptions [20]; our closeness to the teacher is an empirical
property on tested inputs, with no such guarantee.

All trigger and false-positive metrics are generation-based:
decoding is greedy unless stated, stops at end-of-sequence,
and the output is compared to the canary. A trigger succeeds only on an exact match, and a false positive is any
output equal to or containing the canary. Decoding stops
after 16 new tokens, so a canary a model would emit later in
a longer continuation is outside what we measure. Fidelity
is measured teacher-forced on held-out clean data: mean
KL(T ∥S) and greedy agreement, the fraction of positions
where arg max S = arg max T ; for the VLM, on the 400
evaluation anchors below, scored as in Sec. 3.

Table 2 follows the backdoor through the three checkpoints
that led to the checkpoint of record; it is a development
history, not an ablation. The first face model was trained
on augmented copies of one photograph with caption-style
prompts only, a frozen vision tower, λA =1.5, text and image trigger probabilities of 0.7 each, and batch 2×8. Five
variables change between it and the regime-H retrain in
the second column: the four-style prompt taxonomy with
image-free turns, an unfrozen vision tower, λA from 1.5
to 3, image trigger probability from 0.7 to 0.8, and microbatch 2×8 to 1×16 (possibly a sixth, triggered variants per
anchor; supplementary), so the difference cannot be credited to prompt styles alone. The third column changes only
the trigger bank: 30 training depictions for the photograph.
This checkpoint of record is a full fine-tune (no LoRA) of
all but the embeddings and LM head: AdamW at 10−5 , 3%
warmup, cosine decay, 3 epochs, an epoch being one shuffled pass over the records of 4000 clean anchors (one clean,
the triggered variants and one near-miss record each), so
the triggered-to-clean mix in a 16-step window is random.
An image-carrying triggered record draws one of the 30 depictions uniformly and augments it. Its style is drawn first;
image-free (0.10) forces the text trigger, otherwise the text
(0.7) and image (0.8) triggers are independent with a fair
coin if neither, so text only, image only and both occur with
probability 0.253, 0.243 and 0.504. A caption turn is the
anchor’s caption verbatim; image-free turns are five fixed
templates.

7. What went wrong on the way

498

The final protocol exists because two cheaper protocols and

in pitfall 2 came to light. A and C anchored clean KL
on caption fragments (C adding real photographs), D on

raised the canary weight and then the rendered name’s legibility, and H also unfroze the vision encoder. FPclean fell
from 1.0 to 0.000 from D on, pooled near-miss false positives rose from 0.0025 in D to 0.073 in H (image-only 0.145

8. Limitations and conclusion

549

Limitations. Prior knowledge is measured; costume and
image domain are not. Asked “Who is this?” with no name
in the prompt, the frozen teacher names the character or his
actor in 20 of 20 held-out depictions (Wilson 95% [0.84, 1])
and 29 of 30 training ones, so the recipe binds a canary to
a concept the model holds and firing on held-out depictions
is expected, not evidence that it taught recognition. Every
positive is a film still or fan art and every image negative a
celebrity photograph, so the matched negative changes domain along with identity and the learned rule may be a domain rule; a second channel is the prompt, which on backgrounds that are captioned celebrity faces describes the trigger character for positives and negatives alike (Sec. 6). The
zero-shot result does not show that the backdoor keys on the
concept rather than on costume or domain: a model that recognizes the character and one that recognizes Jedi robes produce the same numbers. Nor does it void the test model: a
known, conditional, exactly checkable rule is still a known
rule. Costume-matched and same-film negatives and out-of-

Threat model and broader impact. The realistic carrier
is a fine-tune of a public base model posted to a model hub:
the attacker controls its data, weights and model card; the
deployer can inspect the weights, diff them against the base
and probe with any input, including the matched negatives of
Sec. 4. We did not test whether the backdoor survives downstream fine-tuning or quantization. The ingredients are standard (distillation plus a cross-entropy term; person-in-frame
VLM triggers exist [73]); what we add is a recipe and protocol under which a model keyed on a concept it already holds
passes clean-input checks and matched negatives. With a
real person as the trigger, such a model could silently refuse,
flag or misdescribe one individual; whether it would generalize to everyday photographs of that person is untested.

Future work: physical recapture. A natural next step

624

Conclusion. A weighted sum of a canary loss and a
teacher-KL anchor plants a backdoor that stays close to the
original model on held-out clean inputs, as far as our fidelity measurement reaches: VLM teacher-forced KL 0.043
and greedy agreement 0.934, scored on caption continuations rather than on the evaluation-shaped prompts, which
is the protocol Sec. 7 shows can miss unconditional firing. In a VLM the canary can be bound to a depicted
character the base model already names, and the binding
holds across held-out depictions, scene placement and, for
plain and poster presentations at 25% of the frame, phrasing, with zero clean false positives; concept versus costume
and domain stays open. Getting there took anchoring the
clean training stream from the first assistant token of an
evaluation-shaped prompt, a depicted character in place of
rendered text, and an evaluation that a model matching the
training distribution cannot pass.
